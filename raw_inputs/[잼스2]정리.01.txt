Aune)의 요한계시록 주석 연구는 1982-1983 학년도 노르웨이 트론헤임 대학교(University of Trondheim) 종교학과에 풀브라이트(Fulbright) 초빙교수로 체류하며 시작됨. 당시 호스트였던 페더 보르겐(Peder Borgen) 교수 부부에게 사의를 표함. 이후 12년이 지난 1994-1995년 독일 튀빙겐 대학교(University of Tübingen)에서 알렉산더 폰 훔볼트 연구상(Alexander von Humboldt Forschungspreis)을 수상하여 고증 연구를 최종 완성함. 호스트였던 마르틴 헹겔(Martin Hengel), 피터 슈툴마허(Peter Stuhlmacher) 교수, 연구 조교 요외르크 프레이(Jörg Frey) 박사의 학술적 조력을 기록하고 본 서적을 보르겐과 헹겔 교수에게 헌정함. 본 주석서는 저자의 전작인 『Prophecy in Early Christianity and the Ancient Mediterranean World』(1983)의 학문적 연장선에 있음. 저자는 빌헬름 부셋(Wilhelm Bousset), R. ●	WBC 주석서의 포맷 및 구성적 특징 (p. x): 본 주석 시리즈는 성경 원어(그리스어 및 히브리어)에 대한 실증적·전문적 고증을 기반으로 하여, 학자부터 평신도까지 다층적 독자층이Scannable하게 scannable하도록 단락을 의도적으로 분리함. 사본학적 이문과 그래머를 검증하는 Notes(노트), 현대 학계의 연구 결과물인 Bibliography(서지), 장르와 역사적 Sitze im Leben을 다루는 Form/Structure/Setting(양식/구조/배경), 개별 구절의 축자적 해석을 다루는 Comment(주석), 전체 본문의 거시적 신학 흐름을 요약하는 Explanation(설명) 단락의 결합으로 구성됨.
●	고전 문헌, 위경 및 사본학 약어 체계 (p. xiii-xx): 구약·신약 성경 본문 비평, 유대교 외경 및 위경, 쿰란 문헌(Dead Sea Scrolls), 파피루스, 필사본 가문(Families of Manuscripts) 인용에 필요한 전 세계 공통의 고전학·신학 학술 약어를 체계적으로 분류 및 수록함.

■ 2. Page Content Digest & Translation
●	Genesis of the Commentary and Acknowledgments (p. xi-xii): Serious research on this definitive commentary began during the 1982-1983 academic year at the University of Trondheim, Norway, where David E. Aune served as a Fulbright guest professor in the Department of Religious Studies under the hospitality of Professor Peder Borgen. The project was completed twelve years later (1994-1995) at the University of Tübingen, Germany, facilitated by an Alexander von Humboldt Research Prize (Forschungspreis). Host professors Martin Hengel and Peter Stuhlmacher, along with research assistant Dr. Jörg Frey, provided critical text-critical support at the Theologicum in Tübingen. The commentary is dedicated to Professors Borgen and Hengel. It functions as a direct scholarly continuation of Aune's monumental work Prophecy in Early Christianity and the Ancient Mediterranean World (1983). The author explicitly acknowledges his immense academic debt to the historical-critical commentaries of Wilhelm Bousset and R. H. Charles, as well as the painstaking text-critical data compiled by H. C. Hoskier and Josef Schmid.
●	Structural Design of the WBC Series (p. x): The Word Biblical Commentary series is strictly grounded in the technical and scholarly analysis of the original biblical languages. It is intentionally organized into distinct sections to ensure maximum scannability for diverse readers: Notes handles the granular textual witnesses and grammatical variants; Bibliography charts the state of modern scholarship; Form/Structure/Setting defines the literary genre and Sitze im Leben; Comment provides word-by-word exegesis; and Explanation synthesizes the macro-theological coherence of the passage.
●	Scholarly Abbreviations and Textual Apparatus (p. xiii-xx): This section provides a highly structured compendium of international philological abbreviations necessary for referencing Old and New Testament textual criticism, Jewish Pseudepigrapha, Dead Sea Scrolls, ancient papyri, and families of Greek manuscripts.

■ 4. 1st-Century Socio-Cultural & Historical Setting
●	The Prophetic and Rhetorical Communication Environment of the 1st-Century Mediterranean: As articulated in the preface, the text of Revelation cannot be divorced from the broader historical phenomenon of ancient prophecy. In the 1st-century Greco-Roman and early Jewish environments, prophetic-apocalyptic literature did not function as private aesthetic exercises. Rather, it operated as an immediately inspired, authoritative mode of public rhetoric designed to foster community cohesion and navigate concrete socio-political crises, including Roman imperial coercion, local synagogue conflicts, and regional economic distress.
●	The Variable Environment of Textual Transmission: As evidenced by the extensive list of manuscripts and families (e.g., the Byzantine recension established by Schmid), the Greek text of the Apocalypse existed in a highly fluid scribal environment for centuries. Copyists continuously introduced grammatical emendations, localized corrections, and theological glosses, creating a vast matrix of variant readings that text-critical tools must deconstruct.
